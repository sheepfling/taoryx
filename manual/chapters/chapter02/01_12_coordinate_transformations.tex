\subsection{Coordinate Transformations}\label{sec:coordinate-transformations}

TAOS performs coordinate transformations with unit vectors rather than explicitly
stored transformation matrices because the unit-vector form is efficient to program.
The two methods are mathematically identical, and the unit-vector notation makes
clear that conversion of a vector from one system to another is a sequence of dot
products.

Every TAOS coordinate-system unit vector is expressed in ECFC coordinates. To
convert a vector from ECFC to another system, the vector is projected onto the unit
vectors of the destination system. For example, the propulsive-force vector is
converted from ECFC to body coordinates by
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \sum\vec F_{\mathrm{prop},b}
  &=
  \begin{bmatrix}
    \hat x_b\mathbin{\cdot}\sum\vec F_{\mathrm{prop},\oplus}\\[0.25em]
    \hat y_b\mathbin{\cdot}\sum\vec F_{\mathrm{prop},\oplus}\\[0.25em]
    \hat z_b\mathbin{\cdot}\sum\vec F_{\mathrm{prop},\oplus}
  \end{bmatrix}\\
  &=
  \begin{bmatrix}
    \hat x_b^{T}\\[0.2em]
    \hat y_b^{T}\\[0.2em]
    \hat z_b^{T}
  \end{bmatrix}
  \sum\vec F_{\mathrm{prop},\oplus}
  =\TaosTransform{b}{\oplus}\sum\vec F_{\mathrm{prop},\oplus}.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:ecfc-to-body-force-transformation}
\end{equation}
The rows of $\TaosTransform{b}{\oplus}$ are the body unit-vector components in
ECFC coordinates.

Reversing the transformation gives
\begin{equation}
  \sum\vec F_{\mathrm{prop},\oplus}
  =\TaosTransform{\oplus}{b}\sum\vec F_{\mathrm{prop},b}
  =\TaosTransform{b}{\oplus}^{-1}
    \sum\vec F_{\mathrm{prop},b},
  \label{eq:body-to-ecfc-force-transformation}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \sum\vec F_{\mathrm{prop},\oplus}
  =
  \begin{bmatrix}
    \hat x_b^{T}\\[0.2em]
    \hat y_b^{T}\\[0.2em]
    \hat z_b^{T}
  \end{bmatrix}^{T}
  \sum\vec F_{\mathrm{prop},b},
  \label{eq:body-to-ecfc-force-transformation-transpose}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \sum\vec F_{\mathrm{prop},\oplus}
  =
  \begin{bmatrix}
    \hat x_b&\hat y_b&\hat z_b
  \end{bmatrix}
  \sum\vec F_{\mathrm{prop},b}.
  \label{eq:body-to-ecfc-force-transformation-columns}
\end{equation}
The inverse simplifies to the transpose because the transformation is orthogonal.
TAOS handles transformations involving its other coordinate systems in the same
way.
