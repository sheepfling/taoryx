\subsection{Rail Launches and Sled Tests}\label{sec:rail-launches}

For rail-launched rockets and sled tests, the acceleration is modified to restrict
motion to the rail direction. The rail or sled-track orientation is supplied through
the guidance rules as a body attitude, and the rail is assumed to be aligned with the
body $\hat x_b$ axis.

The ECFC acceleration components required to keep the vehicle on the rail are
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \ddot x_{\oplus}
      &=\lVert\vec a_{rail}\rVert
        \left(\hat x_b\mathbin{\cdot}\hat x_{\oplus}\right),\\
    \ddot y_{\oplus}
      &=\lVert\vec a_{rail}\rVert
        \left(\hat x_b\mathbin{\cdot}\hat y_{\oplus}\right),\\
    \ddot z_{\oplus}
      &=\lVert\vec a_{rail}\rVert
        \left(\hat x_b\mathbin{\cdot}\hat z_{\oplus}\right).
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:rail-constrained-acceleration-components}
\end{equation}
The acceleration magnitude along the rail is
\begin{equation}
  \lVert\vec a_{rail}\rVert
  =\vec a_{\oplus}\mathbin{\cdot}\hat x_b
   -\lVert\vec a_f\rVert.
  \label{eq:rail-acceleration-magnitude}
\end{equation}
The loss of acceleration caused by rail friction is computed from the static or
kinetic coefficient of friction $\mu_f$ and the total force normal to the rail:
\begin{equation}
  \lVert\vec a_f\rVert
  =\frac{\mu_f\lVert\sum\vec F_n\rVert}{m}
  =\mu_f\lVert\vec a_n\rVert,
  \label{eq:rail-friction-acceleration}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
  \lVert\vec a_n\rVert
  =\sqrt{
      \left(\vec a_{\oplus}\mathbin{\cdot}\hat y_b\right)^2
      +\left(\vec a_{\oplus}\mathbin{\cdot}\hat z_b\right)^2
    }.
  \label{eq:rail-normal-acceleration}
\end{equation}
Thus, the normal acceleration is the component of the total acceleration
perpendicular to the rail, and the friction loss is its magnitude multiplied by
$\mu_f$. When the velocity magnitude is less than \SI{0.001}{\foot\per\second},
TAOS uses the static coefficient; otherwise it uses the kinetic coefficient
(\cref{sec:block-rail}).

A launch-rail mechanism prevents a rocket from moving backward, so TAOS enforces
$\lVert\vec a_{rail}\rVert>0$ for rail launches and holds the vehicle motionless
before motor ignition. This restriction is not applied to sled tests because a sled
may both accelerate and decelerate. Sled tracks are normally parallel to the earth's
surface, making the pre-ignition acceleration along the track zero.
