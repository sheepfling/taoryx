\subsection{Ground Speed and Range}\label{sec:ground-speed}

Ground range $r_s$ is the length of the projection of the flight path onto the
earth's surface. Its derivative is the ground speed $\lVert\vec V_s\rVert$, the
velocity of the point on the surface nearest the vehicle. This point is the
intersection of the extended geodetic $\hat z_{gd}$ axis with the earth's surface.

The function \texttt{compute\_state\_rates} computes the following ground-range
and ground-speed output variables:
\begin{longtable}{@{}>{\ttfamily}p{0.17\textwidth}p{0.19\textwidth}p{0.56\textwidth}@{}}
  range  & $r_s$ & Ground range for the complete trajectory.\\
  grseg  & $r_s$ & Ground range for a trajectory segment.\\
  grmark & $r_s$ & Ground range from a designated trajectory point.\\
  vgr    & $\lVert\vec V_s\rVert$ & Ground speed.\\
\end{longtable}

The ground-speed vector is defined as
\begin{equation}
  \vec V_s=\vec V_{\oplus}+\Delta\vec V_s,
  \label{eq:ground-speed-vector-definition}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta\vec V_s$ is the velocity of the surface point below the vehicle
relative to the vehicle. Equivalently, it is the derivative of the altitude vector:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\vec V_s
  =\frac{d}{dt}\left(h\hat z_{gd}\right),
  \label{eq:surface-point-relative-velocity}
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\vec V_s
  =\dot h\,\hat z_{gd}+h\dot{\hat z}_{gd}.
  \label{eq:surface-point-relative-velocity-expanded}
\end{equation}
The altitude rate is given by \cref{eq:altitude-rate}, and the derivative of the
geodetic down vector by \cref{eq:geodetic-down-unit-vector-derivative}. Substituting
\cref{eq:surface-point-relative-velocity-expanded} into
\cref{eq:ground-speed-vector-definition} and expanding the earth-relative velocity
in geodetic components gives
\begin{equation}
  \vec V_s
  =\left(\vec V_{\oplus}\mathbin{\cdot}\hat x_{gd}\right)\hat x_{gd}
   +\left(\vec V_{\oplus}\mathbin{\cdot}\hat y_{gd}\right)\hat y_{gd}
   +h\dot{\hat z}_{gd}.
  \label{eq:ground-speed-geodetic-components}
\end{equation}
Replacing the geodetic velocity components with the flight-path-angle definitions
from \cref{eq:geodetic-velocity-x,eq:geodetic-velocity-y} gives
\begin{equation}
  \vec V_s
  =\lVert\vec V_{\oplus}\rVert\cos\gamma_{gd}
    \left(
      \cos\psi_{gd}\,\hat x_{gd}
      +\sin\psi_{gd}\,\hat y_{gd}
    \right)
   +h\dot{\hat z}_{gd}.
  \label{eq:ground-speed-final}
\end{equation}
Ground speed is the magnitude of this vector, and ground range is its integral over
time.
