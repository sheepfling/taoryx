\vspace{0.8\baselineskip}

This edition is a maintainable, semantic reconstruction of the December 1995
\emph{Trajectory Analysis and Optimization System (TAOS) User's Manual},
SAND95-1652. It is not a page facsimile or a diplomatic, word-for-word
transcription. Numbered mathematics, figures, tables, examples, the Appendix
variable registry, references, index, and distribution material are mapped to the
source and are represented in editable form. Narrative prose is frequently reflowed
and may be lightly modernized, condensed, or clarified. Three ranked Chapter~4
diplomatic-transcription tranches and a residual fourth tranche restore near-source
wording in forty-two selected regions. The residual pass also classifies the remaining
low-scoring Chapter~4 audit queue so that OCR-damaged tables, equations, and listings
are distinguished from coherent prose still worth reviewing. The edition as a whole
remains a semantic reconstruction rather than a complete diplomatic transcription.

Where the printed source contains an evident mathematical, unit, spelling, or
description error, this edition may use a corrected form. Substantive interventions
are recorded in the repository's editorial metadata and release notes. The 1995 scan
remains authoritative for exact historical wording and appearance.

The extracted \texttt{.tbl} and \texttt{.prb} examples are parser-validated as
documentation fixtures. They have not been executed against the historical TAOS
96.0 program. Likewise, reconstructed trajectory plots reproduce the printed axes,
labels, trends, and relative geometry but were not regenerated with the historical
binary.

The source-text fidelity, residual Chapter~4, fixture-validation, targeted-figure,
and equation-provenance reports distributed with this edition describe the audit
methods, known deviations, and validation boundaries. The equation registry maps all
326 numbered equations to their source pages, LaTeX labels, TeX source locations,
and exact display hashes. This closes transcription and provenance, but it does not
claim that every printed equation has already been implemented as executable code or
individually unit-tested.
